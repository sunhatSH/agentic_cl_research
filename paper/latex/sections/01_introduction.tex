\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Large language model (LLM) agents deployed in production evolve through periodic reinforcement-learning (RL) updates: every so often, the policy is refined with freshly collected interaction data. This is inherently a \textbf{continual reinforcement learning} (CRL) problem---the model must acquire new capabilities while retaining existing ones. Unlike lab training that gathers a full, balanced data distribution in one shot, online data arrives quickly and unevenly, so batches covering all capability domains are rarely available; training therefore proceeds \textbf{sequentially, one capability domain at a time}. This exacerbates \textbf{catastrophic forgetting}~\cite{D1}: as the model updates on one domain after another, later domains systematically erode capabilities mastered on earlier ones.

The mainstream remedy is \textbf{experience replay}~\cite{A2}: while training on new data, a fraction of historical experience is sampled and ``replayed'' into the current batch, and an independent continual-learning loss lets the model rehearse old capabilities. Buffer-based replay has been refined along many dimensions: prioritization, partitioning, compression, generative replay, and adaptive sizing. Most prior work shares an implicit assumption that replayed experiences carry a faithful learning signal. In the agentic, multi-capability-domain setting, an additional challenge emerges: different capabilities can crowd each other out in a flat buffer, and the question of what to replay---how much of which capability---becomes a design problem in its own right.

We design and evaluate a complete training-and-evaluation system for continual RL of agentic LLMs, organized around three contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A \textbf{capability-partitioned nine-bucket experience replay buffer}. Buckets are partitioned by capability/domain rather than difficulty. Each has a fixed capacity cap (allocated by a square-root weighting of task counts) and a protective floor. Eviction is in-bucket only, with no cross-bucket displacement. Replay is sampled across buckets by \textbf{distance} in capability space---the farther a bucket is from the current training batch, the higher its forgetting risk and replay weight. Within-bucket sampling and eviction are pluggable (currently uniform and FIFO).

  \item A \textbf{composite continual-learning objective} injected into GRPO with no framework fork: policy gradient, reverse-KL anchor to a reference policy, experience-replay loss, and an always-on entropy regularizer (guarding against policy-diversity collapse~\cite{B4}). Reward comes from a single frozen external LLM judge scoring four dimensions and aggregating multiplicatively, with safety as a binary $\{0,1\}$ gate for consistent evaluation.

  \item A \textbf{bucket-grouped anti-forgetting evaluation protocol}: one evaluation pass after all domains are trained sequentially, with a post-hoc decay weighting by training recency to quantify the core signal---``earlier-trained buckets forget more''---compared against a same-scale baseline without continual learning.
\end{enumerate}

We implement and validate the system on a Qwen3.5-9B agent across nine capability domains (the design extends to larger backbones), using a Pass$^3$ benchmark~\cite{C1} to characterize both new-capability acquisition and old-capability forgetting.
