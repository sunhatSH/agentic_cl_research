\begin{abstracten}
Deployed agentic large language models evolve through periodic reinforcement-learning updates: every so often, the policy is refined with freshly collected interaction data. This is inherently a \textbf{continual reinforcement learning} problem---the model must acquire new capabilities while retaining existing ones. Unlike lab training that gathers a full, balanced data distribution in one shot, online data arrives quickly and unevenly, so batches covering all capabilities are rarely available; training therefore proceeds \textbf{sequentially, one capability domain at a time}. This exacerbates \textbf{catastrophic forgetting}: as the model updates on one domain after another, later domains systematically erode capabilities mastered on earlier ones.

The mainstream remedy is \textbf{experience replay}: while training on new data, a fraction of historical experience is sampled and ``replayed'' into the current batch, and an independent continual-learning loss lets the model rehearse old capabilities. Around this idea, we design and implement a complete training-and-evaluation system for continual reinforcement learning of agents across many capability domains. There are three core contributions.

First, a \textbf{capability-partitioned nine-bucket experience replay buffer}. Buckets are partitioned by capability/domain rather than by difficulty (difficulty drifts with model competence), obtained by merging the official categories of the evaluation benchmark into nine text-only capability domains. Each bucket has a fixed capacity cap and a protective floor; capacity is allocated by a square-root weighting of task counts, balancing more replay for mainstream capabilities against not starving the long tail. Eviction is in-bucket only, with no cross-bucket displacement, so different capabilities never crowd each other out; replay is sampled across buckets by \textbf{distance} in capability space---the farther a bucket is from the current training batch, the higher its forgetting risk and thus its replay weight.

Second, a \textbf{composite continual-learning objective} injected into GRPO with no framework fork: on top of the policy gradient, we add a reverse-KL anchor to a reference policy, an experience-replay loss, and an always-on entropy regularizer (guarding against the policy-diversity collapse of multi-turn agentic RL). Rewards are scored by a single frozen external LLM judge under one rubric, aggregated multiplicatively as completion $\times$ correctness $\times$ trajectory-quality with safety as a \textbf{binary} gate, which resists reward hacking.

Third, a \textbf{bucket-grouped anti-forgetting evaluation protocol}. After all domains are trained sequentially, a single evaluation pass is run (no intermediate evaluation, to save compute); the evaluation set is grouped by the same buckets, aligned to the training order, and scored per bucket. A decay weighting by training recency is then applied post hoc over this fixed set of scores to quantify the core continual-learning signal---``earlier-trained buckets forget more''---and compared against a same-scale baseline without any continual-learning mechanism.

We implement and validate the system on a Qwen3.5-9B agent across nine capability domains (the design extends smoothly to larger backbones), characterizing both new-capability acquisition and old-capability forgetting with a Pass$^3$ benchmark, and isolate the marginal contribution of the buffer, the replay loss, and the composite objective through controlled ablations.
\end{abstracten}
