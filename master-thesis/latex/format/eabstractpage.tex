\begin{abstracten}
Deployed agentic large language models evolve through periodic reinforcement-learning updates: every so often, the policy is refined with freshly collected interaction data. This is inherently a \textbf{continual reinforcement learning} problem---the model must acquire new behaviors while retaining existing capabilities. In the agentic, multi-turn regime, two failure modes make this hard: \textbf{catastrophic forgetting}, where updates on new tasks erode previously mastered capabilities, and \textbf{Echo Trap}, a collapse of policy diversity in multi-turn agentic RL that drives within-group trajectories toward identical outputs and stalls training. Reinforcement fine-tuning forgets less than supervised fine-tuning, yet it still forgets, and the multi-turn agentic setting introduces failure modes invisible to single-turn studies.

Mainstream anti-forgetting approaches---experience replay and its many variants (prioritized, partitioned, compressed buffers)---share a hidden assumption: that the replayed and online experiences carry a \textbf{faithful learning signal}. We argue that in continual \textbf{multi-turn agentic} RL this assumption silently breaks, and it breaks \textbf{before} any replay or regularization. GRPO, the workhorse algorithm in this setting, estimates a per-sample advantage by normalizing rewards within a group of rollouts of the same query; this estimator is unbiased \textbf{only} when the trajectories differ purely from stochastic policy sampling. Two characteristic agentic effects corrupt this premise at the source: \textbf{within-group environment divergence}, where the execution environments across a multi-query session gradually fork so that the advantage absorbs environment noise accumulating with the turn index; and \textbf{multi-turn premise drift}, where a follow-up query referencing the previous turn's result is fixed statically while the realized state is a policy-dependent random function, so the premise holds only with a probability that drifts as the policy improves---rewarding hallucinated fixes to non-existent problems or penalizing honest answers.

A corrupted advantage cannot be repaired downstream: no replay weighting or KL anchoring rescues a gradient whose sign was already wrong. We therefore reframe continual agentic RL as a \textbf{two-stage} problem---\textbf{produce a clean signal, then consolidate it}---and organize the entire system around this thesis. In the clean-signal half, a sandboxed rollout system spawns each group of slots from a single master template for bit-identical starts and re-aligns the session to the winning trajectory at every turn boundary to eliminate within-group divergence; meanwhile, only the first query of each real session is kept as a seed, and subsequent turns are constructed online by three cooperating agents (observer, questioner, reward), so that every follow-up's premise is grounded by construction, which also yields an adaptive curriculum tracking the policy's capability frontier. In the consolidation half, we apply a four-term continual-learning objective injected into GRPO with no framework fork (RL, reverse KL, replay, entropy); a seven-bucket prioritized replay buffer partitioned by capability rather than difficulty and ranked by anti-forgetting value rather than reward; and a U-shaped, action-block-level replay loss reweighting that concentrates credit on the early branching decisions and the concluding segments of each trajectory. We validate the system on a 27B agent across seven capability domains, quantifying both new-task acquisition and old-task forgetting with a Pass$^3$ benchmark, and isolate the marginal contribution of each component through a controlled ablation suite.
\end{abstracten}
